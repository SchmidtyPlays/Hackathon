Oops, I Clicked it! (Emergency Response)

1. Stop Interacting Immediately

If you accidentally click a suspicious link, stop interacting with the website immediately. Do not enter usernames, passwords, payment information, or other sensitive details. Avoid downloading files or granting permissions requested by the website. Simply opening a suspicious link does not automatically mean your device has been infected, but further interaction can significantly increase the risk.

2. Disconnect if Malware Is Suspected

If a suspicious link triggered a download, launched an unfamiliar program, or caused unusual device behavior, disconnect the affected computer from Wi-Fi or Ethernet to help prevent potential malware from communicating with attackers or spreading across the company network. For workplace devices, follow your organization's incident-response procedures and IT team's instructions.

3. Alert IT or Cybersecurity Immediately

Contact your organization's IT department or security team as soon as possible. Explain what happened, including which link you clicked, whether you downloaded anything, and whether you entered personal information. Early reporting gives cybersecurity professionals an opportunity to investigate suspicious activity, isolate affected systems, and prevent further damage. Do not attempt to hide the incident, as delays can make an attack more difficult to contain.

4. Secure Potentially Compromised Accounts

If you entered a password or other login credentials on a suspicious website, change the affected password immediately using a separate, trusted device. If the same password was used for multiple accounts, update those passwords as well. Enable multifactor authentication (MFA) wherever possible and notify IT so they can investigate unauthorized logins, revoke compromised sessions, and protect company accounts.

5. Scan for Malware and Follow Recovery Procedures

If malware infection is suspected, have your IT team perform appropriate security scans and examine the device for malicious software. Depending on the situation, additional steps may include removing infected files, restoring systems from secure backups, or reinstalling compromised software. A clean antivirus scan does not always guarantee that a device is safe, so follow the security team's instructions before reconnecting it to the company network.

6. Monitor for Unusual Activity

After the immediate threat has been addressed, remain alert for suspicious account activity, unexpected password reset notifications, unfamiliar login attempts, or unauthorized financial transactions. Report any unusual behavior immediately. Cybercriminals sometimes use stolen credentials days or weeks after the original incident, making continued vigilance an important part of recovery.

Key Takeaways: Acting quickly can significantly reduce the damage caused by a phishing attack. Stop interacting, report the incident, secure compromised accounts, and follow professional cybersecurity guidance.
